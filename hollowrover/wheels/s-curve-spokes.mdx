HollowRover wheels use a two-part sinusoidal spoke design: each spoke follows a smooth S-shaped curve from the hub to the rim, creating a structure that bends and rebounds under load rather than transmitting every impact directly into the chassis.

## What S-Curve Spokes Are

In traditional wheel design, spokes often run straight from the center hub to the outer rim. These radial spokes are efficient at carrying tension loads, but they are stiff in compression: when a wheel hits a bump, the spoke either holds rigid or buckles. The S-curve spoke replaces this straight line with a sinusoidal path that introduces intentional flexibility at specific points along its length.

The two-part design means the spoke is not a single continuous curve but is composed of two connected sinusoidal segments. This creates multiple points of curvature reversal along the spoke's span. When the wheel encounters a load, these curved segments act like small leaf springs: they compress slightly, store elastic energy, and then release it as the load passes. The result is a wheel that can deform by a small, controlled amount and then return to its original shape.

## How Curved Spokes Add Compliance

In mechanical engineering, compliance is the opposite of stiffness: a compliant structure yields gradually under force rather than resisting it absolutely. The S-curve spoke geometry achieves compliance through a combination of material behavior and geometric effects.

First, the curvature itself changes how force flows through the spoke. In a straight spoke, compressive force travels directly from the rim to the hub along the spoke axis. In a curved spoke, the same force must resolve into components that act perpendicular to the local tangent of the curve at each point. These perpendicular components create bending moments at the inflection points of the S-shape. Bending is a lower-stress mode than direct compression for thin plastic structures, meaning the spoke can absorb more energy before reaching its yield limit.

Second, the two-part sinusoidal design creates what engineers call a progressive spring rate. At small deflections, the outer curves bend gently, providing soft initial compliance. At larger deflections, the curves flatten and the spoke begins to act more like a straight member, providing increasing resistance. This progressive behavior is valuable for a caregiving robot: small floor imperfections are absorbed smoothly, but the wheel does not collapse under the full weight of the rover, dispenser, and arm.

Third, the curved geometry spreads localized point loads into a distributed load pattern. When a straight-spoke wheel rolls over a sharp object, the spoke directly above the obstacle carries most of the force. In an S-curve spoke wheel, the curved path transfers some of that load to adjacent spokes through the rim, reducing peak stress on any single spoke.

## S-Curve Spokes Versus Straight Spokes

| Property | Straight Radial Spokes | S-Curve Spokes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load path | Direct hub to rim | Distributed through curvature |
| Deflection mode | Mostly axial compression | Bending at inflection points |
| Energy absorption | Low (rigid or buckles) | Controlled spring-like rebound |
| Stress concentration | High at single spoke | Lower, spread across multiple curves |
| Failure mode | Sudden buckling or fracture | Gradual deformation before yield |

The S-curve design trades some absolute stiffness for significantly better shock absorption and failure predictability. For a robot moving at 0.3 m/s across indoor floors, shock absorption is more important than maximum stiffness.

## Slight Flex Under Heavy Pressure

The source document notes that the S-curve spokes flex slightly under heavy pressure but hold. This statement describes the intended operating regime: the spokes are designed to deflect within an elastic range, not to remain perfectly rigid. The slight flex is the mechanism by which the wheel absorbs thresholds and small bumps.

The source does not specify the exact load threshold at which flex becomes excessive, nor does it define "heavy pressure" numerically. What is clear is that the design targets the combined static weight of the rover plus the dynamic loads generated by motion and the payload mass of the medication dispenser and water reservoir. The spokes are intended to remain within their elastic range under these normal operating conditions.

## Connection to the Rest of HollowRover

The S-curve spoke compliance interacts with the rover's passive rocker suspension. The rocker arms handle large wheel displacements caused by significant obstacles, while the spoke flex absorbs smaller, higher-frequency disturbances such as tile grout lines and slight floor waviness. Together, they produce a two-stage suspension system: the spokes provide the first layer of filtration, and the rockers provide the second.

This layered approach matters for SLAM accuracy. The STL-19P time-of-flight LiDAR builds a 2D occupancy grid by accumulating scan matches. If the chassis vibrates excessively, the LiDAR pose jumps between scans, introducing noise into the map. By smoothing out high-frequency floor texture through spoke compliance, the wheel design reduces the vibration amplitude that reaches the LiDAR mount, improving scan consistency.

The spoke design also affects encoder odometry. The Dake DK-37R gear motors have encoders that count motor shaft rotations. If the wheel itself deflects, there is a tiny lag between motor rotation and actual ground contact movement. The rover's state estimation fusion, which combines encoder counts with IMU readings and LiDAR scan matching, is designed to tolerate small discrepancies. The S-curve compliance is calibrated to produce deflections small enough that the fusion algorithm can absorb them without significant drift.

## General Engineering Background

In general, S-curve or S-shaped spring elements appear in many mechanical designs beyond robotics. Leaf springs in automotive suspensions use curved metal strips to support vehicle weight. Flexure hinges in precision mechanisms use thin curved sections to create rotation without bearings. Compliant mechanisms, a field of mechanical engineering, intentionally designs flexibility into structures to replace complex assemblies of rigid links and springs. The HollowRover wheel spokes are an application of this broader principle to a low-cost 3D-printed part.

## Why This Matters

For the elderly user, wheel spoke compliance translates directly into a quieter, smoother-moving robot. A robot that bounces over every floor joint sounds mechanical and intimidating. A robot that rolls smoothly sounds deliberate and calm. The emotional impact of a robot's motion is easy to overlook but central to LifeSphere's design philosophy of not startling or alarming the person being cared for.

## Limits

The source does not specify spoke thickness, spoke count, S-curve amplitude, or material infill percentage. The trade-off between compliance and fatigue life is not quantified. Repeated loading and unloading of the S-curve geometry over months or years could lead to material creep, where the plastic gradually deforms and does not fully return to its original shape. Whether this occurs in practice, and over what timeframe, is not addressed in the source document.
