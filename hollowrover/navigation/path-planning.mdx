HollowRover plans and follows routes through the home using Nav2, the standard ROS2 navigation framework, running at a controller frequency of 20.0 Hz with a forward speed limit of 0.3 m/s and a safety inflation radius of 0.35 meters around every obstacle.

## What Nav2 does

Nav2 is a behavior-tree-driven navigation system for ROS2 robots. It takes a goal pose, plans a path through the occupancy grid generated by SLAM, and then follows that path while responding to new obstacles in real time. The system consists of multiple plugins: a planner computes the global path, a controller generates velocity commands to stay on it, and recovery behaviors handle situations like getting stuck.

For LifeSphere, the key safety parameters are the controller frequency, maximum forward speed, and inflation radius. These values were chosen to prioritize safety and stability over speed in a home occupied by a vulnerable user.

## Controller frequency and reaction time

The controller updates at 20.0 Hz, meaning it issues a new velocity command every 50 milliseconds. In general, higher controller frequencies let a robot react faster to unexpected obstacles or drift from the planned path. For a wheeled platform moving indoors among furniture and possibly pets, this cadence provides responsive corrections without overloading compute resources.

## Speed limit: 0.3 m/s

Maximum forward speed is capped at 0.3 meters per second, roughly the pace of a slow human walk. This limit exists for several reasons. First, an autonomous robot should move predictably in a home where a person may be unsteady on their feet. Second, lower speeds reduce stopping distance, giving the obstacle-avoidance layer more time to react. Third, the rover carries a water tank, medication, and a 6-DOF arm: sudden acceleration could spill contents or stress joints.

## Safety inflation radius: 0.35 m

The 0.35 meter inflation radius means every obstacle in the occupancy grid is expanded outward by roughly 35 centimeters before planning. The robot treats inflated cells as walls even though the real object is smaller. This creates a buffer zone. In general, inflation radii are chosen based on robot width, sensor uncertainty, and the need to avoid clipping corners. For HollowRover, this buffer helps prevent contact with furniture legs, door frames, or a person's foot.

## Ultrasonic close-range coverage

Four HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors provide short-range obstacle detection at the front, left, right, and rear of the chassis. Ultrasonic sensors emit sound pulses and measure echo return time to estimate distance. They are complementary to the LiDAR because they detect objects that may sit below the LiDAR scan plane, such as a shoe on the floor or a low step.

The ultrasonic array acts as a close-range safety blanket. If the LiDAR map shows open space but an ultrasonic sensor reports a nearby object, the collision-avoidance layer can override the planned path and slow or stop the rover.

## Why this matters

An elderly person living alone may leave objects on the floor, move a walker, or open a door unexpectedly. Path planning must handle these dynamic changes without human intervention. The combination of SLAM-based global planning, high-frequency local control, conservative speed, wide obstacle inflation, and ultrasonic short-range backup creates a layered safety approach.

## How it connects to the rest of LifeSphere

Nav2 receives its destination commands from the protocol engine. For example, when Vital32 reports a fall, the fall-response protocol sets a goal pose near the user. Nav2 plans a route using the current floor's SLAM map, the rover drives there, and the manipulator arm prepares to assist. The [Mapping and SLAM](/hollowrover/navigation/mapping-and-slam) page explains how the occupancy grid is built and updated.

For person-finding missions outside UWB coverage, the rover switches to BLE RSSI guidance. When the signal strengthens enough to re-enter UWB range, localization resumes and Nav2 takes over path following. See [BLE Person-Finding](/hollowrover/navigation/ble-person-finding).

## Limits

The source does not describe a specific behavior-tree recovery sequence or what happens if the rover becomes permanently blocked. Reverse movement and replanning are standard Nav2 capabilities, but exact recovery logic is not specified.

The ultrasonic sensors have a finite beam width and can miss very narrow objects. They also struggle with soft surfaces that absorb sound. The LiDAR performs best on hard, reflective surfaces; black objects or glass may produce weaker returns.
